## How it works

Mezzotint works backward from every other intaglio method. The printmaker first roughens the entire plate with a rocker, a curved blade lined with fine teeth, rocked across the plate in many directions until the surface is covered in tiny pits and burrs. Inked at this stage, the plate would print a deep, velvety black.

The image is made by smoothing that rough surface. A scraper removes burr, and a burnisher polishes the metal flat. Lightly burnished areas hold less ink and print gray; fully polished areas print white. The printmaker works from dark to light, drawing with light out of the darkness.

Preparing a plate by hand takes many hours, which is why many printmakers buy plates already rocked. Students can learn the dark-to-light approach quickly on materials that roughen easily.

## History

Ludwig von Siegen made the first mezzotint in 1642, a portrait. The method was the first in printmaking built for soft, continuous tone rather than lines. It became the standard way to reproduce portrait paintings in eighteenth-century England, and it was revived by artists in the twentieth century.

## Ages, safety, and setting

Traditional mezzotint uses no chemicals but takes great patience; it suits college and adult students, and committed high school students. The rocker, scraper, and burnisher are heavy, sharp tools that need careful handling.

The dark-to-light idea is easy to teach at younger ages. A plexiglass plate roughened all over with coarse sandpaper, or a juice-box plate scuffed with steel wool, prints dark and can be burnished back with the back of a spoon. A monotype made by rolling a plate solid black and wiping out the lights teaches the same way of seeing with no press at all.

## Plates, presses, and inks

Plates: copper is the classic metal. Pre-rocked plates are sold commercially. Plexiglass and juice-box plates roughened with sandpaper work for teaching.

Tools: a rocker or roulette to roughen; a scraper and burnisher to bring up the lights. The back of a metal spoon or a smooth bone folder can burnish a soft plate.

Presses: an etching press with firm pressure.

Inks: water-washable intaglio ink. The rough plate holds a lot of ink, so wipe carefully to keep the lights clean.
