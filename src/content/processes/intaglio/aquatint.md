## How it works

An etching bath bites evenly into bare metal, and a large bare area simply becomes a shallow pit that won't hold ink. Aquatint solves this by scattering tiny dots of resist across the plate. The etchant bites around each dot, and the plate gains a fine texture that holds ink as an even tone.

The traditional resist is rosin dust, shaken over the plate in a dust box or from a cloth bag and then melted onto the metal with gentle heat. Spray paint misted lightly over the plate does the same job without heating. Tone depth comes from the length of the bite: areas stopped out early print light, and areas left longest print darkest, so a plate can step from pale gray to deep black.

Related methods paint the image directly. In sugar lift, the printmaker paints with a sugar solution, covers the plate with ground, and soaks it in water; the sugar swells and lifts the ground off, leaving the brushstroke open to aquatint. In spit bite, etchant is brushed straight onto an aquatinted plate for soft watercolor-like washes.

## History

Jean-Baptiste Le Prince developed aquatint in France in the 1760s, as a way to imitate wash drawings. Francisco Goya used it for the smoky, shadowed passages of his print series. Picasso was among the twentieth-century artists who used sugar lift, painting directly with the brush.

## Ages, safety, and setting

Aquatint belongs in a ventilated college or adult studio. Rosin dust is a respiratory irritant and an allergen, and in a dust box it can be a fire risk near heat. Spray paint should be used only in a spray booth or outdoors with an appropriate respirator. The etching safety notes apply in full.

Students too young for a studio can still explore the idea. Sandpaper pressed face down onto a soft plate, or a plexiglass plate roughened with sandpaper and printed as drypoint, holds ink as tone in the same way.

## Plates, presses, and inks

Plates: copper or zinc with a safer etchant, or photopolymer plates exposed through an aquatint screen.

Resists: rosin dust, spray paint, airbrushed acrylic, or a sandpaper texture pressed into ground.

Presses: an etching press.

Inks: water-washable intaglio ink wiped with tarlatan. Aquatint tones are delicate, so the final wipe is usually done with paper or the palm.
