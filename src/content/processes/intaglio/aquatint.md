## How it works

An etching bath bites evenly into bare metal, and a large bare area simply becomes a shallow pit that won't hold ink. Aquatint solves this by scattering tiny dots of resist across the plate. The etchant bites around each dot, and the plate gains a fine texture that holds ink as an even tone.

The traditional resist is rosin dust, shaken over the plate in a dust box or from a cloth bag and then melted onto the metal with gentle heat. Spray paint misted lightly over the plate does the same job without heating. Tone depth comes from the length of the bite: areas stopped out early print light, and areas left longest print darkest, so a plate can step from pale gray to deep black.

Related methods paint the image directly. In sugar lift, the printmaker paints with a sugar solution, covers the plate with ground, and soaks it in water; the sugar swells and lifts the ground off, leaving the brushstroke open to aquatint. In spit bite, etchant is brushed straight onto an aquatinted plate for soft watercolor-like washes. <cite>Ross and Romano 1974</cite> At Atelier 17, Stanley William Hayter's sugar lift solution combined sugar with India ink and a little liquid soap. <cite>Heller 1958, p. 161</cite>

A good aquatint covers about half the plate with resist. Printmakers bite a test strip at a series of times, from under a minute up to half an hour, and print it to see the full range of tones before working on the real plate. <cite>Grabowski and Fick 2009, pp. 124–126; Tinsley 1983, p. 50</cite> A test plate only goes so far, though: a large open area of metal bites faster than a small one, so a test two inches square can mislead on a plate a foot across. <cite>Tinsley 1983, p. 50</cite>

Enamel spray paint misted over about half the plate makes an aquatint with no rosin and no heat, and the same spray can be used again as a stop-out for soft gradations. Spray only under an exhaust hood, since even acrylic mists irritate the lungs. <cite>Grabowski and Fick 2009, p. 125; Tinsley 1983, p. 50</cite>

Carborundum grit sealed onto a collagraph gives a similar velvety dark with no acid at all. <cite>Major-George, pp. 13, 35</cite> Heller bit his test steps at 1, 4, and 16 minutes, each four times the last, a geometric sequence a math class will recognize. <cite>Heller 1958, p. 148</cite>

## History

Jean-Baptiste Le Prince developed aquatint in France in the 1760s, as a way to imitate wash drawings. Francisco Goya used it for the smoky, shadowed passages of his print series. Picasso was among the twentieth-century artists who used sugar lift, painting directly with the brush.

<span class="flag">Jan van de Velde IV made tonal etchings in the Netherlands around 1650, and the English painter Paul Sandby gave the process the name aquatint in the 1770s.</span> <cite>Fick and Grabowski, ch. 5</cite> Aquatint was first meant to imitate the look of ink and watercolor washes. <cite>Grabowski and Fick 2009, p. 123</cite>

## Ages, safety, and setting

Aquatint belongs in a ventilated college or adult studio. Rosin dust is a respiratory irritant and an allergen, and in a dust box it can be a fire risk near heat. Spray paint should be used only in a spray booth or outdoors with an appropriate respirator. The etching safety notes apply in full.

Students too young for a studio can still explore the idea. Sandpaper pressed face down onto a soft plate, or a plexiglass plate roughened with sandpaper and printed as drypoint, holds ink as tone in the same way.

## Plates, presses, and inks

Plates: copper or zinc with a safer etchant, or photopolymer plates exposed through an aquatint screen.

Resists: rosin dust, spray paint, airbrushed acrylic, or a sandpaper texture pressed into ground. Rosin can be dusted from a cheesecloth or nylon stocking bag, a salt shaker, or a kitchen sifter in place of a dust box. Salt sprinkled onto a warm ground and later dissolved in water leaves a speckled tone. <cite>Martin, p. 95; Heller 1958, pp. 149 and 152–153</cite>

Presses: an etching press.

Inks: water-washable intaglio ink wiped with tarlatan. Aquatint tones are delicate, so the final wipe is usually done with paper or the palm.
