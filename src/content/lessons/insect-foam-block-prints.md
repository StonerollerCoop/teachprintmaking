Oak National Academy wrote this sequence for Year 3 in England, which is roughly US second grade. It works well through grade 5. If students already made simplified insect drawings for the monoprint lesson, they can reuse them and skip straight to session 2.

## Session 1: Looking and drawing

Show close-up photographs of insects and ask students to find the main body shapes first, then the patterns and textures. Students sketch a few times, choose one drawing, and simplify it into bold outlines with a few strong patterns. Small, delicate details won't survive printing, so keep lines clear and well spaced.

## Session 2: Making the plate and printing

### Making the plate

1. Cut foam plates to size with scissors.
2. Lay the drawing on top of the foam and trace over it with a pencil, pressing firmly enough to dent the foam but not so hard that it tears.
3. Lift the drawing and deepen the lines directly on the foam.

### Printing

1. Roll ink thin and even on the tray.
2. Roll it across the plate.
3. Press the plate firmly onto paper, or lay paper on the plate and rub the back with a flat hand.
4. Peel and look.

Students often expect every part of the plate to print. Show them a print next to its plate and point out that the pressed-in lines and shapes stay blank. That blank space is part of the design.

## Cleanup

Wipe plates gently with a damp sponge or soft cloth if they'll be used again, since running water and scrubbing can bend or snap the foam. Wash brayers and trays with warm water.
