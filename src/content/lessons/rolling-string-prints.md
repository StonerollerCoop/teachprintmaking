## Session 1: Building the roller

1. Give each student a sturdy cardboard tube or a clean can.
2. Trail white glue around the tube in a design: a spiral, zigzags, loops, or waves.
3. Press string into the glue. Cardboard shapes can be glued on too, but keep them all about the same thickness so they all touch the paper.
4. Remind students that the design wraps all the way around, so the end of the pattern meets the beginning.
5. Let the rollers dry overnight.

## Session 2: Rolling the print

1. Brush paint onto the string, or roll ink across it with a brayer.
2. Lay a long strip of paper on a cushion of newspaper.
3. Hold the ends of the roller and roll it steadily along the paper without stopping.
4. Re-ink and roll again beside or across the first row.

## Talking about it

Look at where the pattern repeats. Ask students to find the exact spot where their roller made one full turn. How long is one turn compared with the tube?

## Extensions

Roll a pattern onto plain fabric for a scarf or table runner. Make a class border for a bulletin board with every student's roller printing one section.
