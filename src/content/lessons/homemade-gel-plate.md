## Why make one

Commercial gel plates are firm and give crisp prints. A homemade plate is softer and more painterly, can be cast in any shape, costs very little, and can be melted down and poured again when it tears. Adding glycerin keeps it from drying out, so it lasts for months without a refrigerator.

## Session 1: Casting the plate

You need unflavored gelatin powder, glycerin from the pharmacy, boiling water, and a smooth-bottomed metal or glass pan. A cookie sheet, cake pan, or pizza pan makes a good mold.

1. Put the glycerin in a heatproof bowl and slowly stir in the gelatin powder.
2. An adult adds the boiling water. Stir slowly until the gelatin dissolves. Stirring fast makes foam.
3. Pour into the pans to a depth of at least half an inch. Thicker plates last longer.
4. Pop any bubbles, and drag a strip of paper across the surface to lift away foam.
5. Leave to set for a few hours or overnight.

Recipes vary. Christine Medley's version uses about 12 ounces of glycerin, eight envelopes of unflavored gelatin, and 1½ cups of boiling water, which fills a small cookie sheet and a pizza pan. Halve it for one small plate.

## Session 2: Printing

1. Ease the plate out of the pan onto a sheet of acetate or plastic. The bottom side is the smoothest.
2. Roll a thin layer of acrylic paint across the plate.
3. Lay leaves, lace, string, or cut paper shapes on the paint.
4. Press paper on top, rub with a flat hand, and pull the first print. This one shows the shapes as blank spaces.
5. Lift the masks off, lay down fresh paper, and pull a second print. This "ghost" carries the textures the masks left behind.
6. Let prints dry and layer more colors over them.

## Storing and remaking

Store the plate between two sheets of acetate. When it tears, cut it into chunks, melt it gently in short bursts in the microwave, and pour it again.
