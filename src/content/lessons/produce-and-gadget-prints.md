## Before class

Cut a selection of produce in half: apples across the middle so the star of seeds shows, oranges and lemons for their segments, celery for its crescent, okra and star fruit for their stars, carrots and potatoes for simple rounds. Pat the cut faces dry on a paper towel so the paint sticks. Gather gadgets with flat ends, such as corks, wooden spools, bottle caps, blocks, and clothespins.

Make paint pads by folding a damp paper towel or a thin sponge into a shallow tray and brushing tempera over it. A pad gives an even coat; a puddle of paint gives a blob.

## Steps

1. Show children how to press a piece into the pad, lift it, and press it straight down onto the paper. Then lift it straight up.
2. Let them print freely for a few minutes to learn how much paint and pressure works.
3. Ask them to look at the prints before they look at the produce. Can they guess which fruit made which shape?
4. Invite them to make a pattern: a row that repeats, a border around the edge, or a picture built from shapes, such as a flower from celery petals and an apple center.
5. Try printing one color over another once the first is dry.

## Talking about it

Ask what happened when they pressed harder, or used more paint. Point out that the print is a mirror of the cut face, which matters later when they print letters.

## Cleanup

Compost the produce. Rinse gadgets and paint trays.

## Extensions

Print with fabric paint on a cotton tea towel or tote bag. Print on tissue paper to make gift wrap.
