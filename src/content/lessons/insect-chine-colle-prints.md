## What changes from the original

Before printing, students glue shapes of thin colored tissue onto their paper. The insect plate is then printed over the tissue.

## Steps

1. Cut or tear tissue into shapes that will sit behind parts of the insect, such as the wings or the body.
2. Brush a very small amount of white glue onto the tissue. Too much glue wrinkles the tissue and smears the print.
3. Place the tissue on the paper or card and smooth it flat.
4. Ink the plate, line it up over the tissue, and print.
5. Peel and let dry flat.

Students sometimes think chine collé means gluing paper onto a finished print. Make clear that the tissue goes down first and the print goes on top, so the paper becomes part of the print itself.

## Things to try

Contrasting colors make the insect stand out. Overlapping two tissues makes a third color where they cross. Give students a practice sheet before their final print, since getting the glue amount right takes a try or two.

## Cleanup

Wipe the plate gently with a damp sponge, and wash brayers and trays with warm water.
