## Before class

Buy a fish with clear scales and a good flat side, such as a flounder, snapper, or trout. Rinse it and pat it completely dry, since water keeps paint from sticking. Prop the fins open with small balls of clay or folded paper towel. Set the fish on newspaper.

## Steps

1. Brush a thin, even layer of paint over the fish from head to tail, getting into the fins and tail. Too much paint fills the scales and hides them.
2. Lay a sheet of thin paper, tissue, or a soft paper towel over the fish.
3. Press gently with fingertips over every part of the fish, working from the middle out. Press into the fins and around the eye.
4. Peel the paper away slowly.
5. Re-paint and make another print. Try a second color, or print one fish twice on the same sheet.

## The eye

Traditionally the eye is left unprinted and painted in by hand afterward, which brings the fish to life. Students can add it with a brush once the print is dry.

## Talking about gyotaku

Gyotaku means "fish rubbing" in Japanese. Fishermen in Japan used it to keep a true record of the size and shape of a prized catch, and it grew into an art form of its own. Ask students what a print can record that a drawing might miss.

## Cleanup

Wrap the fish in newspaper and throw it away. Wash brushes, then hands, with soap and warm water.
