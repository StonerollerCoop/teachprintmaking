## Preparation

Cut linoleum into 4 × 6 inch pieces. Tear printmaking paper to about 7 × 9 inches so every print has a margin. Bring in sprigs, leaves, or small potted plants for students to draw from.

## Session 1: Drawing and transfer

Students make two or three quick observational sketches, then choose one and simplify it into shapes that will be either black (left raised) or white (carved away). Point out that the print will come out reversed. Students transfer the drawing to the block by rubbing a soft pencil drawing face down onto the linoleum, or by drawing directly with a marker.

## Session 2: Carving

Demonstrate the V-gouge for outlines and the U-gouge for clearing larger areas. Students fill in the areas that will print with marker before carving, so they can see what stays. Pull a quick proof at the end of the session with a spoon so students can see what still needs carving.

## Session 3: Printing the edition

Roll a thin, even layer of ink on the slab until it sounds like velcro, then ink the block in several directions. Register the block on a simple paper template and print on the press. Each student pulls three good prints, then signs them in pencil with edition number, title, and name.

## Cleanup

Water-based ink washes off blocks, brayers, and slabs with warm water. Wipe blocks dry so the linoleum does not swell.

## Assessment and reflection

Ask students to compare their best and weakest print and explain what caused the difference. Look for clear contrast, controlled cuts, and clean margins.

## Extensions

Print on colored or patterned paper, or carve a second block for a background color.
