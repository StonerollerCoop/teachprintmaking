Libby Clarke's notes for this site point out that the print stands between the hand and the paper, which takes away some of the pressure drawing can carry. The process also asks students to notice their conditions: how humid the air is that day, which paper they are using, how the ink is behaving. That attention makes them both more aware and more playful.

## The Studio Habits of Mind

Researchers at Harvard's Project Zero watched strong visual arts classes and named eight habits those classes build. <cite>Hetland and others</cite> Printmaking exercises each of them.

- **Develop craft.** Rolling ink thin and even, cutting at a steady angle, and lifting paper cleanly are skills that improve visibly with practice.
- **Engage and persist.** A reduction print can't be undone, since every cut removes the block for every color still to come. Students learn to stay with a long project.
- **Envision.** A printmaker has to imagine the print before it exists, including reversal and the order of colors.
- **Express.** Bold relief lines and blunt stencil shapes lend themselves to strong statements, which is why prints have so often been used for protest and celebration.
- **Observe.** Students compare the plate with the print, and one proof with the next.
- **Reflect.** Proofs are a record of decisions. Ask students to write on each one what they changed and why. <cite>Heller 1958, pp. 79, 157</cite>
- **Stretch and explore.** Ghost prints, offset prints, and accidents on the plate often lead somewhere new.
- **Understand art worlds.** Editions, signing and numbering, print shops, and the history of the printed word show students how art reaches people.

## Mistakes stay in

A print often surprises the person who made it, and the surprise is part of the medium. Susie Hodge asks students to leave mistakes in their sketchbooks and even bans erasers so the development shows. <cite>Hodge, pp. 86, 98</cite> Jason Logan's habit of trying a material "to see what happens" and keeping every version of an ink is a good model too. <cite>Logan</cite>

## Planning backward

Printmakers plan from the end. A three-color print needs to be thought through before the first cut, and the lightest color is printed first. That kind of sequencing transfers to any long project, and a simple planning sheet, sketched in the order the colors will be printed, makes it visible.

## Working together

Print shops have always been shared spaces. One student inks while another places the paper; one holds the screen while another pulls the squeegee. Jules Heller began his course with a print every student marked on. <cite>Heller 1958, pp. 244–245</cite> Steamroller printing events, where giant blocks carved by many people are printed with a road roller, turn a whole community into a print shop for a day. <cite>Hughes and Vernon-Morris</cite>
