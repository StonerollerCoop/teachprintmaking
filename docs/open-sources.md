# Open sources for lesson content

Research from October 12, 2026. Every license below was checked on the source's own page unless marked "verify." Nothing here has been imported into the site yet.

## The short version

Openly licensed printmaking lesson plans are scarce. Almost every classroom printmaking lesson online (Art of Education, Lesson Planet, TES, most museum education pages) is all rights reserved. The open material that does exist falls into two kinds:

- **Ready-made lessons.** The largest set by far is Oak National Academy's art curriculum in the UK, which has around fifteen printmaking lessons under the Open Government Licence. The National Park Service has one foam relief lesson in the public domain.
- **Technique books.** Printmaking manuals published before 1931 are in the public domain in the United States. They are rich in technique and history but are not lesson plans, so each one would need to be rewritten into classroom lessons.

So filling the site from open sources will mostly mean adapting and rewriting, with a source credit on each lesson.

## How the licenses sort

| Tier | What we can do | Licenses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adapt freely | Rewrite, combine, and publish with a credit line. | Public domain, CC0, Open Government Licence v3.0, CC BY |
| Adapt with conditions | Rewrite and publish, but the adapted lesson must stay noncommercial, and with ShareAlike it must carry the same license. | CC BY-NC, CC BY-NC-SA |
| Link only | Point to it; we cannot publish a changed version. | Any NoDerivatives (ND) license |
| Ask permission | Not openly licensed. Useful, but only with the creator's written permission. | All rights reserved, or no license stated |

ShareAlike has a practical consequence: if we adapt a CC BY-NC-SA lesson, that page has to be published under CC BY-NC-SA too. Each lesson file will need its own license and source fields so the site can display the right terms per lesson.

## Images

Teacher photos are the long-term plan. Until then, these sources allow reuse. Every image file on the site should record its source link, creator, and license, so the credit can print under the image.

| Source | License | What to watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [The Met Open Access](https://www.metmuseum.org/en/hubs/open-access) | CC0 on works marked "OA" | Thousands of historical prints: woodcuts, etchings, lithographs, screenprints. Only use images that carry the OA mark. |
| [Art Institute of Chicago](https://www.artic.edu/open-access/open-access-images) | CC0 on works marked "CC0 Public Domain Designation" | No download button means the image is not public domain. |
| [Cleveland Museum of Art](https://creativecommons.org/2019/01/23/cleveland-museum/) | CC0 for public domain works (verify per image) | Same pattern as the Met. |
| Smithsonian Libraries (Tokuno, above) and other Smithsonian Open Access items | CC0 | Check each item's rights line. |
| [Library of Congress](https://blogs.loc.gov/teachers/2011/08/wpa-posters-colorful-messages-in-dark-economic-times/) | Varies; WPA posters are generally free to use | Read each item's "Rights and Access" note. |
| Wikimedia Commons (where Wikipedia images live) | Per file: public domain, CC0, CC BY, or CC BY-SA. Commons does not accept NonCommercial or NoDerivatives files. | English Wikipedia also hosts some images under "fair use," usually modern artworks and book covers. Those are not on Commons and are not free for us to reuse. Only use an image if its file page is on Commons and shows a free license. CC BY and CC BY-SA need the creator's name and license next to the image. |
| [Flickr](https://www.flickr.com/creativecommons/) | Per photo, chosen by the photographer | Use CC0, Public Domain Mark, CC BY, and CC BY-SA freely with credit. CC BY-NC and CC BY-NC-SA work only while the site stays noncommercial. Skip anything marked NoDerivatives, since we will crop and resize. |
| [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/license/) | Pexels License | Free to use and edit, and credit is optional, though we will credit anyway. It does not allow showing identifiable people in a bad light or implying endorsement. Search results include some sponsored stock photos from other sites that are not under the Pexels license. |

Two cautions apply to every image source:

- **Photos of artworks.** A photo of a print by a living or recently deceased artist can carry two copyrights, the photographer's and the artist's. A CC license from the photographer does not clear the artwork. Historical prints from museum open access collections avoid this problem.
- **Photos of children.** Classroom photos on Flickr or Pexels may show identifiable students. Prefer hands, materials, and finished prints over faces.

## Notes for using these

- **Credit lines.** Oak asks for title, "Oak National Academy," a link to the lesson, and "Open Government Licence v3.0 (OGL)." NPS asks for credit to the National Park Service.
- **Third-party images.** Oak and NPS both exclude third-party images from their open terms. Adapt the text and take photos ourselves.
- **Oak content dates.** Only Oak's Collection 2 (published after September 1, 2022) is under the OGL. The units listed here are from the current curriculum, but each lesson should be checked for the current Oak footer before adapting.
- **US public domain.** Anything published before 1931 is public domain in the United States. UK rules differ, so a book can be free here and still protected there.
- **Year groups.** UK Year 2 is roughly US first grade; Year 3 is second grade; Year 5 is fourth grade; Year 8 is seventh grade.

This list is research, not legal advice. Any source marked "verify" needs a second look before we publish an adaptation.
